abstract: >
  How does early interaction relate to the way the brain represents facial expressions?
  We analysed EEG recorded while nine-month-old infants and 30-month-old children watched
  dynamic expressions. By 30 months, neural response patterns reliably reflected the
  constituent facial movements, known as action units. At nine months, the strength of
  this representation varied with how often mothers had mirrored their infants’ facial
  expressions in face-to-face interactions at two months.
summary: "Early caregiver mirroring was associated with later neural representations of facial movements in infancy."
